## The brief

> Show, with a worked simulated example, that you understand what actually
> happens between placing a stock order and owning the shares.

This is the smallest piece of graded work in the course, and it stays that way
on purpose: it exists to catch a shaky grasp of accounts, order types and
exchange mechanics early, before Strategy Lab asks you to reason about risk on
top of them. Marks are for explaining and applying these mechanics in your own
example, not for restating definitions.

## What you submit

A written notebook, 500–800 words, structured as four sections:

1. **Account and order type** — which kind of account you're trading from
   (cash or margin) and which order type you used (market or limit), and why
   that order type fit the situation.
2. **The trace** — your simulated order followed from placement to fill: the
   bid/ask you saw, the price it filled at, and why it filled there rather
   than somewhere else.
3. **What could have gone differently** — one concrete way the same order
   could have filled at a different price or not filled at all, explained in
   plain language rather than restated definitions.
4. **Why it matters** — one sentence connecting this trace to the account
   type you chose in section 1.

All prices, quotes and trades are simulated and must be labelled as such
throughout.

## Before you submit

Check that your trace is your own simulated order, not a hypothetical one,
and that every number in it can be pointed back to something you actually
saw on the simulated platform.
