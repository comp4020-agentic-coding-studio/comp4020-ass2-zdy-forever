## The brief

> Write the rulebook you would actually hand yourself before risking real
> money — not a survey of everything the course covered.

The Personal Market Rulebook is where the semester's weights concentrate,
because it is the one piece of work that asks you to commit to a position on
every instrument covered — stocks, short selling and leverage, futures,
options and crypto — rather than describe each in the abstract. It should
visibly draw on the calculations and plans you built across the whole
semester, not restate the lectures.

## What you submit

A single rulebook document, 1200–1800 words, covering:

- **Time horizon** — the holding periods you'd actually use, and why, in
  your own words.
- **Permitted and prohibited instruments** — for each of stocks, leverage/
  short selling, futures, options and crypto, whether you'd use it and under
  what conditions, or why you'd rule it out entirely. "I would avoid this"
  is a valid, complete answer for any instrument.
- **Leverage policy** — the leverage, if any, you'd allow yourself, and the
  margin/margin-call reasoning from Weeks 8–9 behind that limit.
- **Maximum risk** — your position-sizing rule (Week 5's method, reused, not
  reinvented) and an account-level cap that holds even when more than one
  position is open at once.
- **Entry and exit logic** — how you decide to get in and out, referencing
  the observation/assumption/entry/invalidation/exit structure from Week 5
  onward.
- **Review or journalling process** — how and when you'd check whether the
  rulebook itself still holds up.
- **Testing an idea before real capital** — how you'd try a new idea against
  simulated data or a small trial before committing real money to it.

No real trading history or performance claims — this is a statement of
rules, not a track record, and it should stand on its own without you there
to explain it.

## Before you submit

Check that at least one rule visibly traces back to a specific calculation
or plan you produced earlier in the semester — a rulebook that could have
been written in Week 1 hasn't drawn on the course.
