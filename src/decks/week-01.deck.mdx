# Welcome to the Market

## Week 1 · Market overview and basic asset classes

## The question this week answers

What is a market actually doing when it "sets a price" —

and why does this course spend twelve weeks moving from a single stock
order to leveraged derivatives, instead of teaching every instrument at
once?

## A market, defined

A **market** is any standing way for buyers and sellers to meet and agree
on a price.

- a stock exchange
- a futures or options venue
- a cryptocurrency app

None of them guarantee a "correct" price. Each only produces the price the
last willing buyer and seller agreed to.

## The numbers

<div class="calc-block">
  $1,000 budget ÷ $37.50 a share = 26.67 shares
  <br />
  <br />
  Shares trade in whole units → 26 shares
  <br />
  <br />
  26 × $37.50 = $975.00 spent
  <br />
  <br />
  $1,000 − $975.00 = $25.00 left over, uninvested
</div>

That leftover cash is an unglamorous, recurring fact of trading whole
shares — it comes back properly when Week 5 covers position sizing.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>A market is a matching mechanism, not an oracle.</li>
  <li>Five asset classes, one semester, increasing complexity.</li>
  <li>Holding period and instrument complexity are what this course tracks.</li>
  <li>Everything from here is about what happens around that matched price.</li>
</ol>
