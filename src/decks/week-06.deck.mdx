## The plan, all six parts

<div class="comparison-grid">
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Observation → Assumption</h3>
    <p>Three-week uptrend, MA sloping up → uptrend continues</p>
  </div>
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Entry → Invalidation</h3>
    <p>Buy pullback at $48 → daily close below $45 fails the zone</p>
  </div>
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Risk → Exit</h3>
    <p>Sized at 1% of account → target $54, or invalidation, first to hit</p>
  </div>
</div>

---

## The numbers

<div class="calc-block">
  $10,000 account, 1% risk → 10,000 × 0.01 = $100 budget
  <br />
  Stop distance: 48 − 45 = $3 → 100 ÷ 3 = 33.3 → 33 shares
  <br />
  <br />
  Target $54: reward/share = 54 − 48 = $6
  <br />
  Reward: 33 × 6 = $198 vs. risk: 33 × 3 = $99 → roughly a 2:1 R-multiple
</div>

The R-multiple doesn't say the trade will work — it says what's being
sought relative to what's being risked if it does.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>Swing trading holds positions for days to weeks — longer than Week 7, shorter than Week 3.</li>
  <li>A moving average describes a trend already forming, not one about to start.</li>
  <li>A stated invalidation and a Week 5-style size are what make a plan, not the indicator alone.</li>
  <li>Week 7 keeps this same six-part structure and compresses the holding period to hours.</li>
</ol>
