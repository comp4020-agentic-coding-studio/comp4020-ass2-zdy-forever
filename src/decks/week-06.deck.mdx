## The question this week answers

What happens to a carefully sized swing trade when price can reopen beyond
the planned stop before the account has a chance to act?

The point is not to find a "best" trading style or instrument. It is to
understand what can make the account fail.

## A stop controls the intended exit. An overnight gap can control the actual one

Swing trading reduces intraday noise but introduces a risk day trading
avoids entirely — the market can reopen beyond the planned boundary.

---

## A worked setup

A **simulated** watchlist entry, "Fernbridge Retail": a three-week uptrend,
20-day moving average sloping upward.

---

## The numbers: the original sizing still works — until execution skips the stop

<div class="calc-block">
  $10,000 × 1% = $100 risk budget
  <br />
  Planned stop distance: 48 − 45 = $3 → 100 ÷ 3 = 33.3 → 33 shares
  <br />
  Planned risk: 33 × $3 = $99
  <br />
  <br />
  Overnight gap to $43: realised distance = $5/share
  <br />
  33 × $5 = <strong>$165</strong> — before any further slippage
</div>

The gap is simulated. It illustrates why a stop level is not identical to a
guaranteed maximum loss.

<ol class="recap-list">
