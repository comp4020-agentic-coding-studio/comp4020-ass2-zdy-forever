# There Is More Than One Way to Trade

## Week 3 · Investing and trading styles

---

## The question this week answers

What actually separates "investing" from "trading" —

and why isn't one of them simply the correct approach?

---

## By the end of this lecture

- use holding period as the organising axis for the rest of the course
- compare investing, swing trading and day trading on the same two axes
- explain why a style is a fit-to-goals question, not a best-versus-worst
  one

## Holding period, the axis this course tracks most

How long a position is typically kept changes which costs matter, which
risks dominate, and how much attention a position needs.

A position held for years behaves differently, mechanically, from one held
for hours — independent of which one is "smarter."

## Style is a fit question — goals, time available, temperament — not a ranking

---

## A worked example

Three **simulated** students each start with $10,000:

- one buys a diversified fund, checks the account twice a year
- one opens and closes a handful of positions a month, holding each a
  week or two
- one opens and closes within the day, holding nothing overnight

Same capital. Three entirely different sets of concerns.

---

## The numbers

<div class="calc-block">
  trades per year ≈ 250 ÷ average holding period (days)
  <br />
  <br />
  180-day hold (investing): 250 ÷ 180 ≈ 1.4 trades / year
  <br />
  5-day hold (swing trading): 250 ÷ 5 = 50 trades / year
  <br />
  1-day hold (day trading): up to 250 trades / year
</div>

A rough estimate, not a rule — but shorter holding periods multiply how
many times Week 2's execution costs get paid.

## Try it yourself

<p class="activity-label">Classify</p>

A **simulated** trade log entry reads: "bought Monday, sold the following
Wednesday."

Classify it as investing, swing trading or day trading using holding
period alone. Then note what else you'd need — goal, position size, how
it fits a broader plan — before judging whether it was sensible.

---

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>Holding period is the axis, not a proxy for skill.</li>
  <li>Investing, swing trading and day trading suit different goals and time budgets.</li>
  <li>Shorter holds mean Week 2's costs and Week 5's risk tools get applied more often.</li>
</ol>
