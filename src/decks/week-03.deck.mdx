## The question this week answers

How does the same $10,000 account face a different survival problem when
positions are held for years, days, or hours?

The point is not to find a "best" trading style or instrument. It is to
understand what can make the account fail.

## Holding period doesn't tell you whether a trade is smart — it tells you which failure mechanisms deserve the most attention

---

## Same capital, different exposure

Three **simulated** students each start with $10,000:

- one buys a diversified fund, checks the account twice a year
- one opens and closes a handful of positions a month, holding each a
  week or two
- one opens and closes within the day, holding nothing overnight

Same capital. Three entirely different sets of concerns.

---

## The numbers

<div class="calc-block">
  trades per year ≈ 250 ÷ average holding period (days)
  <br />
  <br />
  180-day hold (investing): 250 ÷ 180 ≈ 1.4 trades / year
  <br />
  5-day hold (swing trading): 250 ÷ 5 = 50 trades / year
  <br />
  1-day hold (day trading): up to 250 trades / year
</div>

A rough estimate, not a rule — but shorter holding periods multiply how
many times Week 2's execution costs get paid.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>Holding period changes the mix of risks; it is not a proxy for skill.</li>
  <li>Shorter holding periods multiply how often Week 2's execution friction is paid.</li>
  <li>Swing trading adds overnight exposure; day trading removes that exposure but magnifies intraday friction.</li>
  <li>Weeks 4-8 build a survival toolkit around these changing risk regimes.</li>
</ol>
