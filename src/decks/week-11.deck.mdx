## The same job, different hours

A cryptocurrency exchange performs the same core job as Week 2's stock
exchange — matching buyers and sellers.

It typically operates 24/7 with no scheduled close, and often with far less
standardisation between platforms than regulated stock exchanges have.

This distinction changes what "the position is safe" actually means — before
any question of price.

## The same leverage mechanism, a different name

Leverage on crypto exchanges is often offered at ratios far beyond a stock
broker's margin account — same underlying logic as Weeks 8-9: more leverage
means a smaller price move triggers forced closure.

Here that forced closure is usually called a **liquidation**, not a margin
call — the identical mechanism, a new label.

---

## A worked example

A **simulated** trader opens a long position on a cryptocurrency priced at
$30,000, using 10x leverage — required margin is one-tenth of the position's
value.

---

## The numbers: liquidation price

<div class="calc-block">
  liquidation price ≈ entry price × (1 − 1 ÷ leverage)
  <br />
  <br />
  30,000 × (1 − 1 ÷ 10) = 30,000 × 0.9 = <strong>$27,000</strong>
</div>

A simplified estimate, ignoring fees and funding costs.

---

## Ten times more leverage, a much smaller margin for error

<div class="risk-callout">
  <span class="risk-callout-label">A 10% drop liquidates this 10x position</span>
  <p>Week 8's 2x-leveraged stock example needed a 33.3% drop to trigger a margin call</p>
  <p>The leverage ratio here is five times larger — the required move is much smaller</p>
</div>

Real platforms' formulas include fees and maintenance-margin buffers this
simplified version leaves out — the core relationship holds regardless.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>A cryptocurrency exchange matches buyers and sellers like Week 2's stock exchange, but runs 24/7 with less standardisation.</li>
  <li>Custody — who actually controls the asset — has no stock-market equivalent and changes what "safe" means.</li>
  <li>Liquidation is the same forced-closure mechanism as a margin call, at leverage ratios a stock broker rarely offers.</li>
  <li>Week 12 turns every instrument from this semester, crypto included, into a personal set of rules.</li>
</ol>
