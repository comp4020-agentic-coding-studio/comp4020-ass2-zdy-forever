## Same core mechanics, harsher edge cases

**24/7 market** — no scheduled close; exposure and liquidity conditions can
change continuously.

**Custody** — holding an asset "in the app" can mean the exchange controls
it, not the trader.

**High leverage** — larger ratios shrink the adverse move required to force
a closure.

**Liquidation** — the same forced-exit logic as a margin call, often
automated by the exchange.

## Higher leverage turns ordinary volatility into a forced-exit problem. Crypto isn't a new set of finance laws — it's a venue where this semester's execution, leverage and custody risks show up in more extreme form

---

## A worked example

A **simulated** trader opens a long position on a cryptocurrency priced at
$30,000, using 10x leverage — margin is one-tenth of the notional exposure.
Ignoring fees and maintenance buffers, a simplified teaching estimate places
liquidation near $27,000.

---

## The numbers: liquidation price

<div class="calc-block">
  liquidation price ≈ entry price × (1 − 1 ÷ leverage)
  <br />
  <br />
  30,000 × (1 − 1 ÷ 10) = 30,000 × 0.9 = <strong>$27,000</strong>
</div>

A simplified estimate, ignoring fees and funding costs.

---

## Higher leverage shrinks the survival distance

<div class="risk-callout">
  <span class="risk-callout-label">Entry $30,000 → liquidation ≈ $27,000</span>
  <p>10x leverage leaves only about a 10% adverse move before forced closure, in this simplified example</p>
  <p>Real platforms add maintenance margins, fees and funding — the structural relationship still holds: more leverage means less room for error</p>
</div>

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>A cryptocurrency exchange matches buyers and sellers like Week 2's stock exchange, but runs 24/7 with less standardisation.</li>
  <li>Custody — who actually controls the asset — has no stock-market equivalent and changes what "safe" means.</li>
  <li>Liquidation is the same forced-closure mechanism as a margin call, at leverage ratios a stock broker rarely offers.</li>
  <li>Every survival mechanism this course covers shows up here at its most extreme.</li>
</ol>
