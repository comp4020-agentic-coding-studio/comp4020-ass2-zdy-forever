# Risk Before Strategy

## Week 5 · Risk, drawdowns, stops, position sizing and expected value

---

## The question this week answers

Why teach risk management before teaching any actual trading style?

---

## By the end of this lecture

- explain what a drawdown does to the gain needed to recover from it
- distinguish a stop from an invalidation
- calculate a position size from account size, risk tolerance and stop
  distance
- calculate the expected value of a simulated trading plan

---

## A drawdown isn't symmetric

<div class="risk-callout">
  <span class="risk-callout-label">Simulated account</span>
  <p>$10,000 → loses 50% → $5,000</p>
  <p>To get back to $10,000, that $5,000 must now grow by <strong>100%</strong>, not 50%.</p>
</div>

The same percentage loss and gain are not mirror images of each other — the
larger the loss, the more disproportionate the recovery.

## The numbers: position sizing

<div class="calc-block">
  Risking 1% of a $10,000 account:
  <br />
  10,000 × 0.01 = $100 risk budget
  <br />
  <br />
  Entry $48, invalidation-based stop $45 → stop distance $3/share
  <br />
  100 ÷ 3 = 33.3 → 33 shares (rounded down)
  <br />
  <br />
  Actual risk: 33 × $3 = $99, not the full $100
</div>

Position size answers "how many shares" from the account and the stop —
never a gut feeling.

---

## The numbers: expected value

<div class="calc-block">
  A simulated plan: wins 40% of the time for $150, loses 60% for $80
  <br />
  EV = (0.40 × 150) − (0.60 × 80) = 60 − 48 = <strong>$12</strong>
  <br />
  <br />
  Same win rate, sizes flipped: wins 40% for $80, loses 60% for $150
  <br />
  EV = (0.40 × 80) − (0.60 × 150) = 32 − 90 = <strong>−$58</strong>
</div>

Win rate alone says almost nothing about whether a plan is worth trading.

---

## Same win rate, opposite outcome

<div class="comparison-grid">
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Plan A: +$12 EV</h3>
    <p>40% win rate. Average win $150, average loss $80.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Plan B: −$58 EV</h3>
    <p>40% win rate. Average win $80, average loss $150.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Identical win rate. Opposite sign. The size of wins versus losses is what
decides it.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>A drawdown demands a disproportionately larger gain to recover from.</li>
  <li>A stop is where you exit; an invalidation is why.</li>
  <li>Position size comes from account size and stop distance, not instinct.</li>
  <li>Win rate alone doesn't determine expected value — win/loss size does.</li>
</ol>
