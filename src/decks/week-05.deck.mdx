## The question this week answers

how it wants to trade?

## Four pieces of the survival toolkit

**Drawdown** — a loss measured from a previous account peak. Larger losses
require a disproportionately larger recovery.

**Invalidation** — the reason a trade idea is no longer valid; a stop is
simply where you exit once that reason is confirmed.

**Position sizing** — sets the number of shares from the risk budget and
the stop distance, not from conviction.

**Expected value** — combines win rate with win/loss size; win rate alone
is not enough to judge a plan.

## A 50% drawdown is not repaired by a 50% gain

Risk management isn't something bolted onto a strategy afterwards — it
determines whether the account remains able to take the next trade at all.

<div class="risk-callout">
  <span class="risk-callout-label">Simulated account</span>
  <p>Starts at $10,000 → a 50% loss reduces it to $5,000</p>
  <p>Returning from $5,000 to $10,000 requires a <strong>100%</strong> gain on the remaining capital.</p>
</div>

## The numbers: position sizing

<div class="calc-block">
  Risking 1% of a $10,000 account:
  <br />
  10,000 × 0.01 = $100 risk budget
  <br />
  <br />
  Entry $48, invalidation-based stop $45 → stop distance $3/share
  <br />
  100 ÷ 3 = 33.3 → 33 shares (rounded down)
  <br />
  <br />
  Actual risk: 33 × $3 = $99, not the full $100
</div>

Sizing controls the loss on any single attempt — never a gut feeling.

---

## The numbers: expected value

<div class="calc-block">
  A simulated plan: wins 40% of the time for $150, loses 60% for $80
  <br />
  EV = (0.40 × 150) − (0.60 × 80) = 60 − 48 = <strong>$12</strong>
  <br />
  <br />
  The same 40% win rate, with larger losses relative to wins, can still
  produce a negative EV.
</div>

Sizing controls the loss per attempt; expected value describes the
long-run average of the plan.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>Large drawdowns are disproportionately hard to recover from.</li>
  <li>A stop is where you exit; invalidation is why the idea is wrong.</li>
  <li>Position size comes from account risk and stop distance, not conviction.</li>
  <li>Expected value and loss size matter more to survival than win rate alone.</li>
</ol>
