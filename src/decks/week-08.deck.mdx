# Short Selling, Margin and Leverage

## Week 8 · Leverage arrives, on the simplest instrument it applies to

---

## The question this week answers

What does it mean, mechanically, to trade with borrowed money — and at
what exact price does that borrowing force you out of a position?

---

## By the end of this lecture

- explain how a margin loan works on an ordinary stock position
- explain why a short position's risk profile differs from a long
  position's
- describe leverage as a multiplier applied to both gains and losses
- calculate the price at which a simulated margin account triggers a
  margin call

---

## Margin and leverage

**Margin** — borrowing part of a position's cost from a broker, using the
position (and other account equity) as collateral.

**Leverage** — the ratio between total position size and the trader's own
money in it. $10,000 position, $5,000 own + $5,000 borrowed = 2x leverage.

Leverage doesn't change the instrument — it changes how much a given price
move affects the trader's own capital.

price falls; the loss has no ceiling if it rises instead.

## The numbers: a margin call trigger

<div class="calc-block">
  $10,000 position: $5,000 own money + $5,000 margin loan (2x leverage)
  <br />
  Maintenance margin requirement: 25% of position value
  <br />
  <br />
  Equity = position − 5,000 (the loan stays fixed)
  <br />
  Call triggers when: position − 5,000 = 0.25 × position
  <br />
  0.75 × position = 5,000 → position = $6,666.67
</div>

$6,666.67 ÷ $10,000 = 66.7% of the original value — a **33.3% price drop**
triggers this call, well before the position is worth zero.

---

## The numbers: an uncapped short loss

<div class="risk-callout">
  <span class="risk-callout-label">Simulated short, 100 shares at $20</span>
  <p>Price doubles to $40 → buy-back costs $4,000 → $2,000 loss (100% of proceeds)</p>
  <p>Price triples to $60 → buy-back costs $6,000 → $4,000 loss (200% of proceeds)</p>
</div>

There is no price at which the loss stops growing — unlike a long position
bought outright.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>Leverage multiplies both gains and losses relative to the trader's own capital.</li>
  <li>A margin call is the mechanical consequence of leverage, not an edge case.</li>
  <li>A short position's loss has no ceiling, unlike a long position's 100% cap.</li>
  <li>Weeks 9 and 10 apply this same logic to instruments where leverage is built in.</li>
</ol>
