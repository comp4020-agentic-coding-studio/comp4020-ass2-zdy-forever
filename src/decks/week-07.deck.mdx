## Liquidity and slippage

**Liquidity** — how easily an order fills near the quoted price without
moving it. High liquidity: large orders barely move price. Low liquidity:
even modest ones can.

**Slippage** — the gap between the expected price and the price actually
filled. It grows exactly when liquidity is thin.

## VWAP: a reference, not a signal

**VWAP** (volume-weighted average price) — the average price traded so far
today, weighted by volume. Unlike Week 4's moving average, it resets every
day.

Used here as a reference point for where the day's activity has clustered —
not a buy or sell signal on its own.

---

## A worked morning

A **simulated** stock trades three price/volume pairs in the first hour:

<div class="calc-block">
  $50.00 at 1,000 shares
  <br />
  $50.10 at 1,500 shares
  <br />
  $49.90 at 2,000 shares
</div>

A trader who bought at the day's open quoted at $50.00 gets filled at
$50.04 — a four-cent slip from thin liquidity at that exact moment.

---

## The numbers: VWAP

<div class="calc-block">
  VWAP = [(50.00×1,000) + (50.10×1,500) + (49.90×2,000)] ÷ (1,000+1,500+2,000)
  <br />
  <br />
  = (50,000 + 75,150 + 99,800) ÷ 4,500 = 224,950 ÷ 4,500 ≈ <strong>$49.99</strong>
</div>

---

## The numbers: slippage cost

<div class="risk-callout">
  <span class="risk-callout-label">On a 500-share order</span>
  <p>500 × ($50.04 − $50.00) = <strong>$20</strong></p>
  <p>A real cost paid on entry alone, before the trade has had any chance to work.</p>
</div>

Exactly why Week 8's leverage makes the same mechanics matter even more.

A fill above VWAP paid more than the session's volume-weighted average so
far. A fill below it paid less — a comparison, not a rule about which side
is "correct."
