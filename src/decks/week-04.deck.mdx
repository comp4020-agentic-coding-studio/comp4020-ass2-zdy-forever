# Reading Price

## Week 4 · Candlesticks, volume, trends, support and resistance

---

## The question this week answers

What is a candlestick chart actually recording —

and what can honestly be said about a pattern in one, as distinct from
what a lot of trading content implies about it?

---

## By the end of this lecture

- read what a single candlestick records: open, high, low, close
- explain what volume adds that price alone doesn't show
- define trend, range, support and resistance
- explain the difference between observing a pattern and treating it as a
  signal

---

## What one candlestick records

Four numbers for one time period — this course mostly uses daily candles:

- **open** and **close** — the "body"
- **high** and **low** — how far price moved beyond that before settling,
  the "wicks"

A compression of a lot of individual trades into one shape. Nothing more.

---

## Volume: what price alone doesn't show

**Volume** is how many shares changed hands in the period.

The same price move on high volume reflects a lot of agreement. On low
volume, it might reflect very little. Neither guarantees the move
continues.

## Trend, range, support and resistance describe what already happened. They are observations, never reliable standalone buy or sell signals

---

## The numbers

<div class="calc-block">
  5-day simple moving average:
  <br />
  (51 + 53 + 52 + 55 + 54) ÷ 5 = 265 ÷ 5 = $53
</div>

That $53 is a **lagging** summary — it always sits behind the most recent
move, which is why Week 6 is explicit that a moving average confirms a
trend after the fact rather than calling one in advance.

---

## Observation, not prediction

<div class="comparison-grid">
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Observation</h3>
    <p>Price paused near $51 on this chart.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Assumption (unsupported)</h3>
    <p>Therefore price will pause there again.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Support and resistance describe zones where price has previously paused or
reversed — not exact lines, and not a promise about what happens next.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>A candlestick is a compression of one period's trading — four numbers, nothing mystical.</li>
  <li>Volume adds how much agreement backed a move.</li>
  <li>Trend, range, support and resistance are a shared vocabulary, not a rulebook.</li>
  <li>Weeks 5–7 build risk and entries on top of this vocabulary.</li>
</ol>
