## The question this week answers

What can a chart honestly tell us about where risk boundaries might sit —
without pretending that past patterns predict the next move?

The point is not to find a "best" trading style or instrument. It is to
understand what can make the account fail.

---

## By the end of this lecture

<ol class="objective-list">

## Observation before assumption

<div class="comparison-grid">
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Reasonable observation</h3>
    <p>A mild uptrend with one down day.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="comparison-card">
    <h3>Unsupported leap</h3>
    <p>"Therefore the next day must continue higher."</p>
  </div>
</div>

The risk-useful question is a different one: what future price action would
invalidate the current interpretation?

---

## The numbers

<div class="calc-block">
  5-day simple moving average:
  <br />
  (51 + 53 + 52 + 55 + 54) ÷ 5 = 265 ÷ 5 = $53
</div>

That $53 is a **lagging** summary — built from past closes, so it confirms a
trend only after that trend has already been forming.

---

## A boundary, not a guarantee

A chart can help define "this idea is wrong below here." It cannot guarantee
"my stop will fill here."

That distinction matters most when price gaps or liquidity disappears — the
planned exit level and the executed exit price can diverge.

## The takeaway

<ol class="recap-list">
  <li>Candlesticks and volume describe what happened; they do not predict the next candle.</li>
  <li>Support and resistance are zones that can help define invalidation.</li>
  <li>A planned stop price is a risk boundary, not a guaranteed execution price.</li>
  <li>Week 5 turns these boundaries into position sizes and account-level risk.</li>
</ol>
